# Development history: profile, skills and evaluation

[Japanese](../ja/development-history.md) · [Sources](../README.md)

An edited account of the three supplied implementation records. Historical requests are not replayed as new instructions.

## 1. A personal technology history

The profile starts with a voluntary description, then grows when the person chooses a new major technology. Self-reported ability is not an objective grade. Asking AI to push a commit, moving quickly or skipping a lesson does not establish ignorance. Journals, selected technologies and personal explanations can become local cross-project assets for the same user. Suggestions follow the person's chosen amount, effort and timing.

## 2. Two-stage skills

Reusable AI procedures are drafts first. The person chooses what to understand, keep as a reference or delegate. AI procedure counts are not human mastery counts. A bingo-like board offers optional paths, not deficits or compulsory completion.

## 3. Capture while implementing

An explanation produced only after completion can lose important detail. The design preserves assumptions, alternatives, failure conditions and tool observations during work, with sources, then offers summaries or detailed explanations. Missing history must not be reconstructed from a technology name as though it were observed.

## 4. Connections and provenance

External harnesses, OSS sandbox launchers, MCP, Obsidian, skill imports and model handoff are extension boundaries. Unobserved external effects must not be labelled “main project unchanged”. Importing a procedure does not prove the person's learning.

## 5. Twenty evaluation projects

The proposed suite covers ordinary requests, contextual fixes, Ajax, read-only diagnosis, numeric contracts, permissions, migrations, compatibility, model switching and reflection failure. Model completion claims and independent outcomes are separate. This page does not report twenty passing projects.

## 6. Publication

CLI remains primary. Pages introduces the interaction, verantyx.ai explains the project, and My Atlas is a private view of personal records. Demo recordings use only prepared public fixtures. Earlier renaming and testing instructions remain history, not current commands.
